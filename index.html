Tongyu(Tory) Wang

Researcher in Mechanics and Robotics

I work at the intersection of mechanics, sensing, and computation. My research focuses on medical and continuum robotics, force inference, physics-based simulation, and Sim2Real control.

Current focus: shape-environment fusion for continuum-robot force sensing and control of magnetic robotic systems for vascular intervention.

Research

My projects combine analytical models, numerical simulation, estimation, and physical prototyping. The emphasis is on understanding the mechanics well enough to make sensing and control more dependable.

Georgia Institute of Technology

Research Assistant, remote

Apr 2026 - present

Shape-environment fusion-based force sensing for continuum robots

I am developing a method to infer distributed contact forces from robot shape and environmental constraints. The work combines Coulomb-friction contact in a Cosserat-rod model with constrained EKF/MAP estimation, fusing FBG shape sensing and vision in a closed-loop simulation and validation pipeline.

  • Cosserat-rod mechanics
  • Contact and friction modeling
  • Constrained state estimation
  • FBG and vision sensing

RuggedizedWrist: distal mobility for contact-rich tool use

I am developing a compact two-degree-of-freedom wrist for a Flexiv Rizon 4s. My work spans mechanical design, Isaac Lab comparison of 7- and 9-DoF manipulators, WebXR teleoperation, and tactile-vision sensing with a CoinFT sensor and an optical silicone dome.

Sim2Real control for magnetic robotic intervention

I built ROS/Gazebo workflows for a magnetic robotic arm, evaluated PRM* motion planning with Cartesian interpolation, and developed continuum-robot simulations in SOFA. The current work studies reinforcement-learning transfer from simulation to a physical system for vascular intervention.

Active drops in surfactant systems

Under the supervision of Prof. Corinna Maass, I studied high-Peclet-number transitions in active drops. I implemented CFD simulations in C++ with Basilisk and used Python to analyze results, evaluate governing equations, and verify reduced physical models.

Beijing Shidai Heavy Storage Technology

Research Intern

Sep 2025 - present

Mechanics of slope-based gravity energy storage

I develop unequal-height catenary models and numerical solvers for slack and sag in mountain-slope cyclic transmission systems. This work supports mechanical analysis and layout optimization for long-distance, high-load energy storage infrastructure.
